# Design guidelines

Every design decision in the clinic inventory manager follows the [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines) (HIG) and the HealthOS design guidelines (`HealthOS/docs/design-guidelines.md`), which record the HIG sentences behind each house rule. This document records only what this app adds or decides differently. Check a page against both before calling it done.

Reference pages: Inventory and the Stock In / Stock Out dialogs were hand-refined first and are the model for everything else.

## 1. Lists and tables

Sources: Lists and tables; HealthOS §7.

- **One line per row, every table** (user ruling 2026-09-24, https://claude.ai/artifact/CjeJH1TiBegeUnEtxTBPKX). A cell never stacks two lines. Body rows are a fixed 48 px, set once on `table-body.svelte` (`[&>tr]:h-12`); cells are `px-3 py-2`, so a 32 px button or input fits exactly and a text-only row still measures the same. Pages add no `py-*` to cells. Skeleton rows inherit the height, so a placeholder table is the loaded table with bars.
- **A second line goes to one of four places**, in this order of preference:
  1. **Its own column** when it is a fact people scan or sort by: the request time became a sortable Requested column ("Today 10:32", "19 Sep 16:05"); the order badge and non-order reason on Inventory became an Order column, matching Price List and the Dashboard.
  2. **Inline after the figure** when it qualifies that figure: a small muted or tinted phrase, `text-xs` with `ms-1`, in the same cell. "23 Sep 2026 14:05", "24 Jun 2025 1 year ago", "12 Oct 2026 in 18 days" (`expiryNote()` in `utils/expiry.ts`, warning or destructive ink; `expiryBadge()` stays for dialogs). An overwritten payroll run is a neutral badge beside its saved time. A batch row reads "Batch 1 · received 14 Jan 2026".
  3. **A badge after the name** when it is the row's own state and only the exception needs marking, as HealthOS marks only an inactive patient beside the name: Inventory and Price List show Low stock, Out of stock and Not ordering after the item name and nothing for In stock, and the stock figure beside it stays in plain ink: HIG Color asks that colour never be the only carrier of a fact, and the badge already says it. Where every row is an exception (the Dashboard's To Order tab) the state has its own Status column, a badge like the Status column on Waiting for Delivery (user ruling 2026-09-27, replacing the dot, so the two Dashboard lists match).
  4. **Dropped** when another column or control already says it: the batch count (the chevron shows the batches), "No stock" (the badge says Out of stock), "Since {date}" on Employees (the date the record was added, not a hire date), and any explanatory sentence ("Stock Out takes the earliest expiry first").
- **An item the clinic has stopped buying is "Not ordering"** (user ruling 2026-09-27; the field is still `not_track`). It leaves every Dashboard list and the low and out of stock counts, and holds no order status, but its stock is still counted and Stock In and Stock Out still work; "Not tracked" read as if counting had stopped. Its saved reorder level shows as a dash until it is ordered again. Inventory and Price List both carry the Not ordering badge after the name, so the plain zero explains itself. It is the only switch: the Low-stock alert checkbox did the same job, was never used, and went, so a reorder level is always zero or more.
- **The Dashboard's lists carry one Status column and no column that is mostly empty** (user ruling 2026-09-27, https://claude.ai/artifact/JiFvpb6bPSvvXY97hA6wqv). To Order lost its Note column, which held only a snooze and was a dash on nearly every row: the snooze badge and its reason follow the stock badge in Status. Waiting for Delivery lost Received: "4 received, 6 to come" follows the ordered figure, in warning ink, only once something has arrived. Its Status is one badge, the most urgent that applies: Late, then Back-ordered, then Partly received, then On the way; Expected is then a date, or a muted "No date yet". The order date and snooze date drop the year (`formatDayMonth`); Expected keeps it.
- **No row stripes.** A row's state is the badge or dot in the row, never a coloured inset on the leading edge as well; the two said the same thing.
- **Names stay whole.** An item or person name never wraps or truncates in a table: the cell keeps the primitive's `whitespace-nowrap` and `Table.Root` scrolls sideways when it must, as every HealthOS table does. Pages add no `whitespace-normal`, `break-words`, `max-w-md` or `min-w-48` to name cells.
- **Remarks and notes truncate.** Secondary free text gets one line: the column head carries a percentage width, the cell is `max-w-0`, and the text sits in an inner `div.truncate` with the full text as `title` (table cells ignore `max-width` otherwise). The edit dialog still shows all of it. This is HealthOS's `max-w-xs truncate` with `title` on notes, widened to a column. The exception is the Price List remark, which shows in full under the item name and has no column of its own (user ruling 2026-09-28, https://claude.ai/artifact/1q7W2KRNDGTHjNx2a6gMLh): the purchaser reads it while ordering, and a Remark column wide enough to read pushed the order buttons off a 1920 px screen. It is a `div` in the Item cell, muted, `whitespace-pre-line wrap-break-word` so long lines wrap and typed line breaks are kept, and `w-0 min-w-[max(100%,16rem)]` so it takes the width the names have set without widening the column. Only a row with a remark is taller than one line. The list is not sorted by remark. The row's buttons keep their words: icons were weighed and refused, since nothing pictures Mark Ordered or Not now.
- **Column heads are sentence case in the body face, never all caps.** `table-head.svelte` carries HealthOS's head style (`text-foreground h-12 px-3 font-medium`, the table's `text-sm`), and `SortHeader` renders the same text with only the arrow added, so a fixed column beside a sortable one does not look like a different table. Plus Jakarta Sans sets capitals tightly, so an all-caps label reads cramped; that, and HealthOS's "No all-caps labels", rules out uppercase eyebrows anywhere (stat-card labels are plain `text-xs` muted text). Sortable heads sort what the column shows; when a column goes, its sort key goes with it (Inventory no longer sorts by status; the Low Stock and Out of Stock segments cover that).
- **Names are capitals, set with tracking** (user ruling 2026-09-27). Item, unit, supplier and employee names are kept in capitals, so one name has one spelling: the name fields raise the letters as they are typed (the value itself, not a display transform) and the server raises them again (`capitalName` in `convex/lib/names.ts`), which covers an Excel sheet. Plus Jakarta Sans sets capitals tightly, so wherever such a name or a unit code is shown it carries `tracking-wide`: name cells, picker rows, `Quantity`, `DialogSubject`. A name field adds `placeholder:tracking-normal`, since its placeholder is a sentence. Search fields are left as typed; they match in any letter case. The HIG does not rule on the case of data; Writing asks only that capitalization be chosen and applied consistently, and interface wording here stays sentence or title case.
- **A stock figure is `Quantity`** (`components/app/Quantity.svelte`): the number in the normal ink and medium weight, the unit code muted beside it, the pack size ("(100 TAB)") muted and `text-xs`, since it describes the item, not the count. "13 BTL (100 TAB)" set in one weight read as a single lump; the number is what a person came for. A second figure of the same item on the row (Reorder level, a batch row, On hand on Approvals) passes `pack={false}` so the pack size appears once. Tone goes on the number through `valueClass` (destructive when out, warning when low, success or destructive with the sign on Movements). Text figures that are not stock (money, counts) stay plain `tabular-nums`.
- **Dates and times in cells**: `formatDate` ("23 Sep 2026") for a date, `formatDayMonth` ("20 Sep") inside a badge or beside a time so it never pushes the next column, `formatTime` as the small qualifier after the day. Figures are `tabular-nums`.
- **Connection status is a quiet pill, not an alert.** A dropped Convex socket shows "Reconnecting…" in muted `text-xs` at the end of the top bar (`connection.isOffline`), and only after three seconds down so a blip never flashes it; the client reconnects and replays on its own. HealthOS shows nothing; this app commits stock counts, so a spinner with no explanation would be worse than a word. This is how Gmail, Slack and Linear do it: small, out of the content flow, and named for the recovery.
- **The Dashboard has no stock buttons** (user ruling 2026-09-25, https://claude.ai/artifact/X3fEAj6DtT589z1ZupwCMw). The purchaser and the store clerk share one login, and the purchaser never moves stock, so the Dashboard's rows carry only her decisions: Mark Ordered… and Not now on To Order, Update Order… and Not Ordered on Waiting for Delivery, nothing on Expiring and Not Moving. Stock In and Stock Out stay on Inventory. Mark Ordered asks how many, in the item's unit with the pack size beside the field and no proposed figure (user ruling 2026-09-25: the purchaser knows the quantity, a prefilled one gets confirmed unread); Stock In counts against that quantity and closes the order on its own, so a short delivery shows as "Partly received" rather than dropping the item back into To Order.
- **The Dashboard's headline strip is its tab list** (user ruling 2026-09-25, https://claude.ai/artifact/3MPHQbULSLKFaLn3RF8Urs). The strip and a pill tab bar beneath it were the same four queues with the same glyphs, counts and tones, and both switched the list, so the tab bar went. Each segment is a `Tabs.Trigger` styled as the tile (`variant="line"` on the list, so bits-ui supplies the roles, the roving arrow keys and the active bar): glyph, Title Case label, the count as the big figure, one line of context. The open queue is tinted `bg-muted/50` with a 3 px bar along its bottom edge and no chevron; the others keep the chevron. A zero stays in its fixed place as a muted zero, never hidden, so the four segments never shift. The Show snoozed checkbox and Open Inventory link keep their row above the list, end-aligned.
- **Row text buttons are outline** (user ruling 2026-09-25). HIG Buttons (macOS): "The standard button type in macOS is known as a push button", the bordered one; and "Use style — not size — to visually distinguish the preferred choice among multiple options", with prominence kept to "one or two per view". Neither action on a Dashboard or Price List row is the preferred one, so both are the standard bordered button (`variant="outline"`), including the Not now menu trigger; the filled default button is reserved for the page's primary and a dialog's confirm. This is what every HealthOS table already does: each `size="sm"` text button in a row (Sign-Ups, Updates, Find, Blood Tests, Directory) is `outline`, and `ghost` appears only on `icon-sm` buttons, the More ellipsis and disclosure chevrons. Ghost text stays for a Clear link in a toolbar and a navigation link such as Open Inventory, as HealthOS uses it for Today in the calendar footer. The staff are not familiar with tech, and a borderless word in a table does not read as something to press.
- **A button that starts a delete is tinted red wherever it sits** (user ruling 2026-09-27): `variant="destructive"` on the Delete… row button of Units and Suppliers, on Delete Item… and Delete Employee in an edit dialog's footer, and on the Delete… item of a More menu. The outline rule above is about which action is preferred; this one marks the action that removes something. The confirm button inside the dialog stays the plain default, as `ActionModal` rules, because by then the person has chosen it.
- **Row hover** is kept (`hover:bg-muted/50` on `table-row.svelte`), unlike HealthOS: rows here carry inline buttons and an expandable batch list, so the tint tells a person which row the pointer is on.

## 2. Pickers

Sources: Pickers, Lists and tables; HealthOS §5 (patient picker).

- **Choosing one record is a search field that holds the pick** (`ItemPicker`, user ruling 2026-09-24, after HealthOS's patient picker): a text input with a search glyph; typing shows up to eight matches beneath it; a click, or Arrow keys and Return, chooses one, the list closes and the field becomes a block showing the whole name (wrapping if it must, never clipped in an input) with a clear glyph; a click anywhere on it starts a new search. An empty field means no choice ("Any item" in the Movements filter). The staff who use this app are not familiar with tech, and this is the search box they know from everywhere; a list inside a dropdown button, and before that an always-open list that only ticked a row, did not read as choosing. The New Request dialog and the Movements filter share it.
- **A unit is picked, never typed** (user ruling 2026-09-27, https://claude.ai/artifact/9JeaMmXYzuwsaj5hpoZQaf). The Unit field in Add Item and Edit Item (`UnitField`) reads left to right as a sentence, "BOX contains 30 TAB": a select, the word, a number field, a select. Both selects list the Units page; an empty number means the unit holds nothing countable and switches the second select off. The three parts are stored separately (`unit`, `pack_size`, `pack_unit`) and every page shows them joined as `unit_label`, "BOX (30 TAB)". Free text had produced "BOX (3O TAB)", "BTL (120ML)" and both BOTTLE and BTL. The Excel sheet keeps one text column, read on import and refused when it cannot be read or names a unit that is not on the list.
- **Units is a child page of Inventory, and a rename reaches every item** (user ruling 2026-09-27). It is set up once and rarely opened, so it has no sidebar entry: it sits in Inventory's More menu under the Excel items, and its breadcrumb leads back to Inventory. Each row is the unit, how many items use it, a switch, and Rename…; the switch, "Suppliers price by this unit" (user ruling 2026-09-27), is on for a pack such as BTL and off for an amount inside a pack such as TAB, and it decides only which buttons the Price field offers, since the price's own unit is saved on the item; Delete… appears only on a unit nothing uses. Renaming to a name already on the list combines the two: the dialog's title and confirm change to Combine and the hint under the field says how many items will change, the consequence of the name being typed. Names are one word in capitals, so the field keeps only letters and raises them as typed. The count of items is a link to Inventory held to the items that use the unit, as the unit or as its contents (`/inventory?unit=NAME`, user ruling 2026-09-28). Inventory gets no unit control for it, since nobody browses by unit: the filter shows as a button, "Unit: BOX" with a cross, that removes it.
- **A supplier is picked from a dropdown with a search field** (user ruling 2026-09-28, replacing the typed field of 2026-09-27, https://claude.ai/artifact/VGRuHFEG5RamSrfk8RzLgJ). Each item has one supplier, the one it is normally bought from, in a Supplier field under the item name in Add Item and Edit Item, and in Edit Price Details (`SupplierField`). It is the same control as the Supplier filter (`Popover` with `Command`), as wide as the form: the button shows the supplier, it opens with a search field above the whole Suppliers list, typing narrows it and a click or Return takes one. No Supplier is the first row and is shown as a dash in tables. The typed field had shown only eight names, could hold half a name, and needed the text deleted before another supplier could be seen. The Excel sheet has a `supplier` column; a sheet without the column leaves every supplier as it is, an empty cell clears it, and a new name joins the list, so the first fill needs no setting up.
- **Suppliers is a child page of Inventory, beside Units** (user ruling 2026-09-27). It sits in Inventory's More menu under Units and works as Units does: each row is the supplier, how many items use it, and Rename…; Delete… appears only on a supplier no item uses; Add Supplier… is the page's primary. Renaming to a supplier already on the list combines the two, with the same Combine title, confirm and consequence hint. The count of items is a link that opens Inventory with the Supplier filter held to that supplier (`/inventory?supplier=NAME`, user ruling 2026-09-28): Inventory is already the list of a supplier's items, with search and Edit beside each, so the Suppliers page does not repeat it.
- **The Supplier filter is one dropdown, the same on three pages** (`SupplierFilter`): the Dashboard's To Order, Inventory and Price List. It offers only the suppliers in the list it filters, each with its count, then No Supplier for the items still to be filled in. On To Order that means only suppliers with something to order, so the purchaser picks one and places one order. A choice the list no longer offers, because the supplier's last item was ordered, falls back to All Suppliers rather than leaving an empty list. Supplier is also a column after Item, sortable on Inventory and Price List, shown on Waiting for Delivery so a late order says who to call, and matched by the search field.
- **The Supplier filter is searchable, and the search field still matches suppliers** (user ruling 2026-09-27). The dropdown opens with a search field above the list (`Popover` with `Command`), since a few dozen names is a long list to scroll. The page's search field keeps matching the supplier name as well as the item name. HIG Searching: "Aim to make your app's content searchable through a single location"; HIG Search fields: "Default to a broader scope and let people refine it as they need" and "Use placeholder text to help people know what they can search for", which is why the placeholder reads "Search by item or supplier". The two are not the same control twice: search finds, the filter holds a list to one supplier while she works through it.
- **Price List has no order-status segments** (user ruling 2026-09-27, https://claude.ai/artifact/9JeaMmXYzuwsaj5hpoZQaf). Its All, To Order, On Order and Snoozed segments repeated the Dashboard's queues, so they went: the Dashboard says what to act on, Price List says what is bought, from whom and at what price. Its toolbar is the search field and the Supplier filter. The Order status column and the row's order buttons stay, so a supplier's whole range can still be ordered from here, and sorting by Order status gathers the items on order.
- **Every field in a dialog spans the dialog's width** (user ruling 2026-09-27). A field alone on its row is full width, including a number such as Reorder level; a row of buttons that choose a value (the price unit, the snooze chips) is a full-width grid of equal parts (`grid w-full auto-cols-fr grid-flow-col`), as the toolbar segments are. Two fields share a row only when they are read together: Order date and Expected by, Quantity and Expiry date, Month and Year.
- **A field that picks from a list saves only what is on the list** (user ruling 2026-09-27). No picker accepts free text, so none has a not-on-the-list error: `ItemPicker` holds an item or nothing, `SupplierField` is a dropdown with search, and the Unit, payroll and snooze fields are selects. New suppliers are added on the Suppliers page, and the server refuses a name that is not on the list. Only an Excel import adds a supplier by name, because a sheet holds whole names and is the way the list is first filled.
- **Which picker for which list** (user ruling 2026-09-28). Up to about two dozen short choices: a select. A few dozen names: a dropdown with a search field. Hundreds of records: a search field that holds the pick (`ItemPicker`).
- **The price is typed once, where the order is placed** (user ruling 2026-09-27, https://claude.ai/artifact/9JeaMmXYzuwsaj5hpoZQaf). Mark as Ordered has a Price field (`PriceField`) that opens with the last price; left empty, the last price stays. It is labelled with the unit she orders in, "Price per BOX". Two outline buttons, "Per BNDL" and "Per BTL", appear above it only where the contents are packs a supplier prices; units switched off on the Units page, such as TAB and ML, never get them. One line underneath gives the order total, which is where a wrong button shows. The price is saved as typed (`amount` and `unit`) and shown as typed by `Price`, "RM 12.00 / BTL": Price List has a sortable Price column, sorted by what one of the item's unit costs, and the Dashboard's To Order shows Last price. The Price List pencil is Edit Price Details: Supplier, Price and Remark. Inventory shows no price, since that page is the clerk's. Excel carries `price` and `price_unit`; a sheet without the columns leaves prices alone.
- **On-hand figures in hints are `Quantity` too** ("**1** BTL (1 OZ) on hand.", "**12** BTL left after this stock out."), so the number reads the same everywhere; error lines stay plain destructive text.
- **The unit sits inside the quantity field** as a trailing `InputGroup.Text`, pack size included ("BOX (20 BTL)"), so a typed "3" means 3 of that. The hint and the error repeat the full figure in a plain sentence ("5 BOX (20 BTL) on hand."); the pack size stays out of the picker rows, where it is noise before an item is chosen.
- **A picker row shows the whole name.** The one-line rule is for tables, where a row is a record among peers; in a picker the name is the thing being chosen, so it wraps rather than truncates (`min-w-0 flex-1`, `items-start` on the row, the check icon nudged to the first line). A quarter of item names run past 30 characters and the longest past 60, so a truncated row hid exactly the part that tells two strengths apart. The trailing fact is one compact figure (`Quantity` with `pack={false}`, `text-xs`, destructive when zero) and no word after it; what it means is clear from the field, and the Quantity field's description repeats the on-hand figure once an item is picked.

## 3. Hints and descriptions

Sources: Writing, Alerts; HealthOS §9.

- **A hint stays only when it changes what the person types or shows what will happen.** The live figure under a quantity field ("5 BOX (20 BTL) on hand.", "12 BTL left after this stock out.", "Deducts RM 12.00.") stays: it is the consequence of the number being typed. A sentence that explains how the app works ("filters apply page by page, so the total…"), restates the field ("Min and max, in the item's unit."), or restates the title ("Enter the clinic password to continue." under Sign in) goes (user ruling 2026-09-24). The staff are not familiar with tech, and a sentence they cannot act on is noise they still have to read.
- **A confirm dialog's description says the consequence in plain words**, one or two sentences, no jargon: "That month's saved payroll and its payslips are deleted. This cannot be undone.", not "frozen figures"; "Stock is deducted as soon as you approve."
- **Empty states keep their one sentence** when it says why the list is empty or what to do next ("Every item is above its reorder level or already on order.", "Try another search or clear the filter.").
